## The origin (age 17, Singapore Poly)

**What actually made you try climbing? A friend dragged you, a CCA, saw it online, walked past a gym?**
I've tried climbing before when I was young in primary school camps and a little bit outside, but never really had the opportunity to do climbing as schools didn't have it as a CCA and I was more occupied with sports like track and field and football.

**First impression — were you bad at it? Scared? Hooked immediately? What kept you coming back?**
I loved climbing from the very start before I even joined SPRC (Singapore Poly Rock Climbing), but just never had a circle of friends to do it with. Really enjoy doing different routes.

## The shift to speed climbing (18)

**Why speed specifically, over other climbing types? What drew you to the racing side?**
I loved climbing, especially speed climbing. The first time trying speed climbing I managed to get 10 seconds and it made me hooked instantly, as I kept wanting to come back to get the next timing — 9 seconds, the next day 8 seconds, and so on. I love the process of grinding hard and reaping the fruits of my labour after a long and hard training cycle.

**How did getting picked for Singapore happen so fast — did a coach spot you, did you compete your way in?**
Because I kept coming back to the gym to chase a new personal best every time, I suddenly found myself in a position where I could hit 7 seconds. So I had a friend from the national team who asked me to join the national trials. At the national trials I managed to place 3rd, which allowed me to join the team.

## The false start (your first international race, 18)

**Where was it, what competition?**
It was in India, Asian Youth Championships. It was my first ever international competition, and my first/last youth competition as I was at the oldest age of the youth category.

**What was going through your head on the start pad — and in the seconds after you realised you were disqualified?**
I was very confident; with the amount of work I had put in I was confident that I could place well and qualify for finals. All I had to do was not false start, so I told myself to just have a slow reaction and not worry about it, as I knew I was fast enough to qualify even with a slow reaction. However, a very unfortunate thing happened — I did the same thing I always do in training, my same setup and start, but because the pad there was very sensitive, even though I never left the pad, the small shift of weight in my feet caused the timer to false start. When I heard the false start I instantly thought it was my opponent, as I knew I went after the beep, so I continued my run. But to my horror when I reached the top, I saw the timer saying false start on my lane. I was devastated. When I came down I went to one side and cried.

**What did you do that night / that week? Did you nearly quit? Who or what got you back?**
I cried a lot; I was so sad and disappointed — not just that I couldn't make the finals and climb more, but that I couldn't prove myself worthy of being in the team and show how hard I had trained. I had managed to cut my time all the way down to 6.1 seconds from 7 seconds, but I had nothing to show for it. I did not quit. I was more determined than ever.

**Concretely, what did you change in training afterwards to fix the timing?**
I trained harder than ever, never missing a training. But it wasn't easy — I went back and worked on changing my start to make sure this mistake would never happen again, but it was not an easy task as I had to take two steps back to fix it. I decided there and then that I was going to be the first Singaporean to ever medal on the world stage. I was going to prove myself.

## The comeback year (19, 2025)

**ASEAN (not Asian) Championships bronze — where was it held? What did that medal feel like after the year before?**
It was held in KL, Malaysia. I had mixed feelings about the bronze — on one hand I had medaled at an international competition, proving to myself that I can do it, but on the other I was nowhere close to satisfied with my performance, as I knew what I was capable of and what timings I could do.

**The national record + SEA Games + 4th place — what was the record time, and whose record did you break? Do you remember the moment you saw the result?**
The national record is 5.332 seconds, breaking the previous record of 5.54. I was overjoyed when I saw my time, as I knew I had a slow reaction (I was just making sure I didn't false start and repeat last time's mistake), but even with a slow reaction I still managed to break the national record.

**The "Tips to Stay Tip Top" slide (school specifically wants this)**
I do daily stretches before and after training, and a lot of foam rolling. I watch what I eat — I make sure not to take too much sugar and track my protein intake, always hitting my protein goals for the day.

**Your real recovery / nutrition / sleep habits — what do you actually do to stay in shape?**
I train 5–8 times a week, hit at least 120g of protein a day, stretch after trainings, and try to sleep early.
